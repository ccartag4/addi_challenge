## A. Assumptions (business definitions and interpretation)

| # | Assumption | Rationale | Alternative considered |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **A real person is identified by `document_number` alone.** | The data dictionary states it explicitly. | `document_number + country`: 346 of the 907 shared documents span CO and BR with different birth years (F13). Both counts are published in `RESULTS.md` Q7; the dictionary rule is the primary answer. |
| A2 | **An application's final state is its latest event by `event_at_utc`**, with `_ingested_at_utc` as tiebreaker and, for identical instants, `_op` order I < U < D. | The dictionary defines current state as the latest *version*; business time is the event time, ingest time only says when we saw it. | Latest by `_ingested_at_utc` (gives a different final status for 54 applications, per exploratory profiling; confirmed in staging tests). |
| A3 | **Any application with a `_op = 'D'` event is invalid** and excluded from every metric, together with its loans (none exist) and events. | Dictionary: a deleted application did not exist for business purposes. | None. |
| A4 | **`customer_id = 999999999` is a placeholder, not a customer.** It is treated as NULL, and an application's customer is the real `customer_id` seen on any of its events. | It is the only customer id absent from the master, appears on 1,353 events across CREATED/APPROVED/REJECTED and I/U/D, and 104 of its rows are exact twins of a real-customer row (F4). | Keep it as an unknown customer: would break referential integrity for 1,348 applications and mis-assign 325 loans whose latest event carries the placeholder. |
| A5 | **A valid loan** has an application that exists in the CDC, is not deleted, and whose final status is APPROVED. | Dictionary: loans without a valid application are corrupt migration records. | None. |
| A6 | **Every business date is the UTC timestamp converted to `America/Bogota`.** Disbursement month, payment date, application date and FX lookup date all use the Bogotá date. | README section 4.2. | UTC dates: shift 5,900 loans by a day and 171 by a month (exploratory profiling; re-measured in `dq_09`). |
| A7 | **FX rate for a date = last published rate on or before that date** (forward fill over a daily calendar). GMV uses the rate of the disbursement business date. | The provider publishes on business days only; 176 days per currency are missing (F12). | Backward fill or interpolation: not how treasury books a rate; forward fill is the standard "last known rate". |
| A8 | **`legacy_v1` amounts are in minor units for both COP and BRL and are divided by 100.** | Median settled payment ÷ median installment is 100.0 for legacy in both currencies and 1.0 for core_v2 (F9). | Apply the factor to one currency only, or use 1,000: contradicted by the measured ratio. |
| A9 | **An effective payment is a `SETTLED` row not referenced by any `REVERSED` row.** Reversal rows are never money; a payment voided by several rows is excluded once. | Dictionary semantics of reversals. The rule is implemented by *set membership* (is this payment a reversal target?), not by summing signed amounts, so it would stay correct if a payment were ever reversed twice (F11). | Netting positive and negative amounts: fragile to duplicated or repeated reversal rows and breaks the "one row per effective payment" grain the README asks for. |
| A10 | **Payments duplicated by timestamp format are one payment.** Deduplication is on `payment_id` after parsing timestamps. | 666 `payment_id`s have two rows that differ only in the string format of the same instant (F3). | None. |
| A11 | **Outstanding balance = sum of `amount_due` of every installment not settled as of the date**, whether overdue or not yet due; a partially paid installment counts in full. | README 4.2 defines it literally as "the sum of its unsettled installments". A net-of-partial variant is exposed alongside (`outstanding_net_of_partial`) so the difference is visible. | Net of partial payments: arguably closer to an accounting balance, but not what the definition says; published as a secondary column, not as the metric. |
| A12 | **Outstanding balance is converted to USD at the FX rate of the snapshot date** (forward-filled calendar, A7). GMV keeps the disbursement-date rate. | A balance is a position on a date; it is valued at that date's rate. GMV is a flow booked on the disbursement date. | Disbursement-date rate for the balance: mixes rates from many dates into one position and would make PAR30 depend on when each loan was originated. |
| A20 | **`fct_installment_status` states each installment AS OF `snapshot_date`** (payments with Bogotá date on or before it). Columns suffixed `_any` use every effective payment, so a month-end series can be derived without re-running the allocation. | Days past due and "settled or not" are only defined relative to a date; making it explicit (`as_of_date` column, one project var) keeps the snapshot, FPD30 and PAR30 on one definition. Data ends on 2026-07-01, one day after the cut; a single payment is affected. | Build "as of the latest data" and derive the snapshot afterwards: the same numbers here, but the as-of would be implicit. |
| A21 | **FIFO allocation by interval overlap.** Payments ordered by `paid_at_utc` then `payment_id`; installments by `due_date` then `installment_number`. A payment funds an installment by the overlap of its cumulative-money interval with the installment's cumulative-debt interval. Money beyond the plan total is left unallocated. | Exactly the README rule (oldest payment settles oldest outstanding installment, one-to-many both ways) without recursion: window functions only, auditable pair by pair in `int_payment_allocation`. | Recursive CTE or procedural loop: same result, slower, and harder to test. |
| A22 | **`settled_date` is the Bogotá date of the payment that completes the installment.** | Business dates are Bogotá dates (A6); the settling moment is the last allocation that brings `paid_amount` to `amount_due`. | UTC date: would move some settlements one day and mis-count late payments at day boundaries. |
| A24 | **An installment is overdue from the day after its due date.** On the due date itself it is *due* but not *past due*: DPD = 0, it does not count in `n_overdue` or in the overdue amount, and the loan is `CURRENT`, not `DELINQUENT`. | Consistency with the DPD definition ("days elapsed since the due date"): a gold test `(dpd > 0) = (n_overdue > 0)` failed for 164 loans whose only unpaid installment was due exactly on 2026-06-30. Counting them as overdue with 0 days would make "overdue" and "DPD > 0" disagree. | Treat due-today as overdue: inflates the 1–30 bucket's loan count with zero-day cases; PAR30 and FPD30 (both > 30 days) are unaffected either way. |
| A23 | **Days past due accrue until settlement if settled, until the as-of date otherwise; never negative. FPD30 denominator = first installments with `due_date <= as_of_date - 30`; numerator = those with `days_past_due > 30`.** | README 4.2 wording ("more than 30 days", "already reached the 30-day mark"). Early payments give `days_to_settle < 0` but `days_past_due = 0`. | Denominator with `due_date + 30 < as_of_date`: differs by the loans due exactly 30 days before the cut, which can never be flagged; the README says "reached", so ≤ is used. |
| A13 | **PAR30 in `agg_merchant_monthly` is a stock at the month end:** outstanding USD of the merchant's loans with DPD > 30 at that month end, over the merchant's total outstanding at that month end, from `int_loan_month_end_status`. FPD30 stays a cohort metric by disbursement month. A cohort view of PAR30 as of the snapshot is exposed as `par30_cohort_rate_at_snapshot`. | "Portfolio at Risk" is a position, and a monthly series of it is what a merchant risk review reads. It is cheap because FIFO settlement dates are cut-independent. At the snapshot month end the series reproduces `dm_loan_delinquency_snapshot` loan by loan (singular test). | Cohort only: simpler, but "PAR30 in March" would then mean "how the March vintage looks today", which is not a portfolio metric and cannot be compared across months. |
| A25 | **`merchant_category` in `agg_merchant_monthly` is the SCD2 category in effect on the last day of the month.** Rows where the merchant changed category inside the month carry `category_changed_in_month = true` and `category_at_month_start`. | The grain is merchant × month, so one category per row; the period close is the conventional reference and equals the event date for the month-end stock metrics. The exact event-date category remains in silver (`fct_loan.merchant_category_at_disbursement`) for any category-level analysis that needs it. | Split the row by category (grain merchant × month × category): honours the event date for flows but breaks the requested grain and leaves the stock metric with no natural home in the earlier-category row. |
| A14 | **An application belongs to the Bogotá date of its first CDC event (creation).** That date drives its month in `agg_merchant_monthly` and the merchant category in effect for it. Loans use their disbursement date. | Creation is the moment the merchant generated the application; the decision date is not available for the few applications never decided, and approval rate per month should compare applications of the same vintage. | Decision date: shifts applications that take days to decide into the next month and mixes vintages in the approval rate. |
| A19 | **A loan's customer is its application's resolved real customer**, falling back to the loan's own `customer_id` only when the application has none. | The origination record is the source of truth (A2); 325 loans carry a different `customer_id` than their application's *latest* event only because that event holds the placeholder (F4). The loan's own value is kept in `loan_customer_id`. | Use the loan's value: correct here too (a consistency test proves both agree), but it would silently diverge if the placeholder ever appeared on a loan. |
| A18 | **A person's attributes come from their most recently created customer record** (`created_at_utc`, then `_ingested_at`, then highest `customer_id`); counts and conflict flags describe all of the person's records. | The latest record is what the origination core last captured for that person; older records are kept reachable through `bridge_customer_person` and the `customer_ids` list, so no information is destroyed. | Earliest record: stale contact data. Field-by-field "most complete" merge: harder to explain and to reproduce. |
| A17 | **A merchant version with no category inherits the category of the merchant's earlier version; a merchant that never had one is reported as `UNKNOWN`.** The raw value and the rule applied are kept in `category_source` / `category_imputation`. | The history is append-only and each row records a change: a later row with no category most plausibly changed something else (5 cases, e.g. name casing), not the category. A never-categorised merchant cannot be guessed; `UNKNOWN` keeps its 533 applications and 238 loans in every total instead of dropping them from a join. | Drop the 16 versions: silently loses volume from GMV and approval rate. Fill from the most common category: invents data. |
| A16 | **Customer sentinels become NULL:** `birth_year <= 1900`, `monthly_income < 0`, and non-numeric income such as `N/A`. | 63 rows at exactly 1900 and none between 1900 and 1920 is a placeholder pattern, not a population; `-1` and `N/A` are explicit "unknown" markers from an unvalidated self-declared field. | Keep the raw values: they would poison any age or income aggregate. |
| A15 | **Customer `created_at` is a calendar date stored as a midnight-UTC timestamp; the customer creation date is its UTC date, not the Bogotá conversion.** | All 30,000 values are at `00:00:00` (dq_09: 30,000 of 30,000 rows would shift a day under the Bogotá rule). Converting would date every customer one day earlier than the source shows. No metric in the challenge depends on this field. | Apply A6 uniformly: mechanically consistent but visibly wrong for a business user. |

| F7 | loans, installments | 120 loans whose `application_id` does not exist in the CDC; the same 120 have no installments. | Integrity | Exclude as corrupt migration records (A5). | 120 | `dq_04` rows 6/14; `relationships` test loan → application |
| F8 | loans | Loan attributes agree with the originating application: customer (once the placeholder is removed), merchant, currency, and principal = approved amount for every loan. | Consistency, Accuracy | Keep as enforced tests, they protect the join. | 0 mismatches | `dq_04` rows 8–11; Phase 3 singular test |
| F9 | payments | `legacy_v1` reports amounts in minor units: median payment ÷ median installment = 100.0 for COP and BRL; core_v2 = 1.0. | Accuracy | Divide legacy amounts by 100 (A8). | 21,022 legacy rows | `dq_05` rows 18–21 |
| F10 | payments | `legacy_v1` loan references carry an `LN-` prefix; core_v2 uses the bare id. | Validity | Extract trailing digits as `loan_id`. | 21,022 | `dq_05` row 7; test: every payment's loan exists |
| F11 | payments | 1,296 `REVERSED` rows at string level, each with the negative of its target amount, same source system, no dangling targets. The 5 "double reversals" seen in raw profiling were the same reversal row delivered in two timestamp formats (F3): after typed deduplication there are 1,291 reversal rows voiding exactly 1,291 payments, one each. | Accuracy, Uniqueness | Exclude reversal rows and their targets (A9); the design stays robust to genuine double reversals by counting distinct voided payments. | 1,291 reversal rows · 1,291 voided payments | `dq_05` rows 9–15, `dq_14` rows 3–4; singular tests `assert_no_reversed_payment_in_fct_payment`, `assert_payment_counts_reconcile` |
| F12 | fx_rates | 176 missing days per currency between 2024-12-23 and 2026-08-14: 170 weekend days and 6 holidays (2025-01-01, 2025-04-18, 2025-12-25, 2026-01-01, 2026-04-03, 2026-05-01). | Completeness | Daily calendar with forward fill (A7). | 176 per currency | `dq_06`; test: no loan without a rate |
| F13 | customers | 30,000 `customer_id`s but 29,093 distinct documents: 907 redundant ids (never more than 2 per person). Of the 907 shared documents, 346 span two countries, 884 have different birth years once the 1900 sentinel is nulled (886 raw), only 4 share an email. Under the alternative identity `document + country` there would be 29,439 people and 561 redundant ids. | Uniqueness | Person grain on `document_number` (A1); `has_cross_country_ids` and `has_conflicting_birth_year` flags on `dim_customer`; both counts reported in `RESULTS.md` Q7. | 907 | `dq_07` rows 3–8, `dq_12`; tests `unique document_number`, `assert_person_counts_reconcile`, `assert_bridge_covers_every_customer_id` |
| F14 | customers | Free-text hygiene: `monthly_income` has `-1` sentinels (858), the string `N/A` (1,505) and thousands separators (3,506); `birth_year = 1900` on 63 rows with nothing between 1900 and 1920; every email needs lower/trim; city has 14 raw spellings for 9 places (`Bogotá D.C.` / `bogota dc` / `BOGOTA` are one city). | Validity | Sentinels → NULL (A16), numeric parsing, lower/trim, alphanumeric matching key plus a canonical city seed. | see `dq_07` | `dq_07` rows 11–18; staging range tests |

Missed by the first profiling pass (it counted distinct values, which hides NULLs) and by staging's `accepted_values` (which ignores NULLs); caught by `not_null` on `dim_merchant.category`. | Completeness | A17: carry the earlier version's category forward, else label `UNKNOWN`; keep `category_source` and `category_imputation` so the fill is visible. | 16 versions · 16 merchants · 574 valid applications · 265 loans on those versions | `dq_08` rows 15–19; `dim_merchant` tests on category, imputation and their consistency |

Legacy honoured the Bogotá business day, core_v2 honours UTC midnight. | Timeliness | No transformation needed; encoded as the singular test `assert_payment_sources_respect_cutover` so a late legacy file or a back-dated core row is caught. | boundary rows only | `dq_14` rows 11–12 |
